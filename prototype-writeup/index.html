DSC 106 Final Project

Are Urban and Rural Drug Mortality Rates Converging Over Time?

Group Members: Govind Sriram, Prisha Maiti, Zachary Louie, Anand Baidya

What we have done so far

So far, we have built the first interactive prototype of our final project, which investigates drug mortality between rural and urban counties in the United States. We binned each county into four population quartiles (smallest, lower-middle, upper-middle and largest) as a proxy for urbanicity. We then converted each county's reported death-rate interval into a single midpoint value for every year in the NCHS dataset, since the NCHS publishes ranges rather than exact rates in order to protect the privacy of counties. For the visualization, we implemented an animated line chart with a year slider and "Play" button which shows the user how the drug mortality of the four different county groups changed from 1999 to 2015.

Most challenging part to design and why

The most challenging part to design will be the scrollytelling layers. We will have to carefully sync up multiple charts to the scroll state on the page without any random jitters. This needs to be combined with proper narration and writing for each section, which means that we will have to proceed with greater exploratory visualizations. We will also have to take into account the positioning and views of the story and charts, because the animations must both draw attention to the story we are trying to convey, as well as present sufficient information to convince the reader of it. For example, we have a mock storyline on our page alongside a chart plotted from real data — we must be careful to annotate only the most important pieces of information that are relevant instead of writing up notes for each year.
